# Cold emails for job and thesis applications

Researched 2026-10-24. Sources: Steve Dalton, *The 2-Hour Job Search* (5-point email; 75-word version in the 2020 edition), 80,000 Hours email scripts, Inside Higher Ed, Cornell and Purdue guides for emailing professors, Hunter, Pin, Scale.jobs, BetterUp. The user writes and sends every email; the assistant only drafts.

## Rules for every cold email

1. **Right person, before applying.** Prefer the named contact, the hiring manager or a team engineer over a generic inbox. Write before or right after applying: once an application is in, some companies limit what hiring managers may say.
2. **Subject names the thing.** The position title, or the team plus the shared link (“MSc student, question about the X team”). Never “Question” or “Application”.
3. **One line that proves you looked.** Name a concrete question from the ad, a recent launch, a paper, a talk or a repo, and why it caught you. No praise words.
4. **One proof, not a CV.** The single strongest, true piece of evidence (with a number if there is one). Everything else is attached or linked.
5. **One small ask.** Consider us; a 15-minute chat; or “who would be the right person to ask?”. Never several asks.
6. **Short.** Body of about 75–125 words, under six sentences. Plain sentences, the user's own register; never a template copied word for word.
7. **Questions only when they earn their place.** Good cold emails often carry one genuine question, and it can be the ask itself (“Would the project be open to two students?”, “Who would be the right person to talk to about X?”). Never a list of questions, never one the ad already answers, never one asked only to seem engaged. **No proposed meeting times**: say you would be glad to meet soon.
8. **Name attachments plainly** (CV, transcript, cover letter), matching the file names.

## Three situations

**A. Applying for an advertised thesis or job by email** (university ads, supervisors). Attach CV, transcript and cover letter when the ad asks for them. Shape: who we are and what we apply for (1–2 sentences) → the line that proves we read the ad → one proof per person → “attached are…” → glad to meet.

**B. Networking with an engineer, alum or team member** (Dalton's 5-point email, for roles without a named contact or before applying). Under about 100 words; lead with the connection (same university, same programme, a talk they gave); **don't ask for a job or a referral in the first email**; ask for their insight in a short call; keep the interest general enough (“how your team works on X”) that saying yes costs nothing. The referral question comes after a conversation, if at all. LinkedIn people search (recruiters, people in the same role) gives the names.

**C. Reaching a hiring manager or recruiter directly** (job ads, after applying online). 75–125 words: the role and one specific signal from the company → one proof with a number → the ask (“I applied for X; I'd be glad to talk”). Link the CV or attach it only if the ad says email applications are fine.

## Follow-up

This is for emails to a person. Applications submitted through a portal follow the 14-day rule in the tracking stage.

- No reply after about a week: one short follow-up in the same thread that adds something new (a finished project step, a relevant result, an answer to something in the ad), not “just checking in”.
- Second and last follow-up one to two weeks later. Then stop; record it in the log.
- Follow-ups are drafted by the assistant and sent by the user, like every email; `03-tracking.md` holds the reminder dates.
