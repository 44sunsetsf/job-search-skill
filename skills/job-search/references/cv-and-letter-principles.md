# CV and cover-letter principles

A short summary of widely used guidance for CVs and cover letters in Sweden and the EU, in the spirit of university career-service advice (it was originally distilled from Chalmers Career Services material; re-read your own university's material if anything is unclear).

## CV

- 1 page preferred, 2 at most. Reverse chronological order. Submit a PDF with a professional file name.
- Simple single-column layout; a multi-column layout makes the reading order unclear to ATS.
- Contact: name, phone, professional email (not the university one), LinkedIn, city. No address, age, gender, nationality or personal ID number. A photo is optional and the CV must work without one.
- **Profile (about 3 lines):** who you are professionally and what you offer this role; show personality and area of interest; tailor it to the ad.
- **Education:** the specialisation and the thesis topic when relevant; relevant courses.
- **Experience and projects:** order by relevance and recency; bullets on the main topics, the effect of the work and the skills used; concrete results, scope and tools, not duties. Show the 2–3 most relevant projects.
- **Skills:** mirror the ad's terminology where accurate. Every listed skill must be one you can demonstrate in an interview and should appear in the experience descriptions.
- **Languages:** state levels clearly and accurately.
- **ATS do:** standard headings, keywords from the ad when they genuinely match, simple bullets and readable fonts, dates, titles, companies and degrees easy to identify. **Avoid:** keyword stuffing, text in images, headers or footers, icon fonts, unusual section names.
- **Five-minute tailoring routine:** (1) read the ad like a recruiter and mark 5–8 requirements; (2) match evidence; (3) reorder and rewrite so the connection is obvious; (4) check ATS readability; (5) final check: “if a recruiter only reads the top half of page one, will they understand why I am relevant?”
- Keep a master CV and tailor a version per application. Relevance beats completeness.

## Cover letter

The core principles:
- Be the solution to their problem; make the company the main character; take the recruiter's perspective.
- Give examples from real experience and explain clearly how you became the person they are looking for, showing both technical and soft skills.
- Keep it to one page.

A typical letter covers: a hook (what in the ad draws you, or how you found it); your education and relevant courses or tools; the key responsibility with a real example and result; a second requirement; a soft skill the ad implies with a brief example; your motivation for this company or topic; a closing with what you hope to learn and a thanks. The number of paragraphs is flexible: merge, reorder, adapt or drop points when your real story reads better that way, and don't force anything in just to match the template. Template phrases (“I was particularly drawn to your emphasis on …”) are fine when they read naturally. The test is whether the whole letter is logical, natural and true.

## Interview tips

- Update the CV; research the role and company; mind eye contact and posture; listen and ask follow-up questions; be yourself and honest about weaknesses.
